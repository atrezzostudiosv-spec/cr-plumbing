Humidity Control for North Texas Homes

DFW gives you both problems in the same year. Summer brings humid stretches where the AC can’t keep up with the moisture, so the house feels clammy at a temperature that should be comfortable. Then winter heating dries the air out, and you get static shocks, cracked wood trim, dry skin and a sore throat every morning.

The target is somewhere between 30 and 50 percent relative humidity. Below that, the dryness problems start. Above about 60 percent, you’re in mold and dust-mite territory and the house feels warmer than the thermostat says. A whole-home system holds the level steady instead of treating one room at a time.

Before we recommend anything, we look at your home: square footage, how tight it is, your ductwork and what your HVAC system can support. Sometimes the fix isn’t equipment at all. If a crawl space is venting damp air into the house or a bathroom fan vents into the attic, we’ll say so. This work sits alongside our other indoor air quality services.

Humidifier & Dehumidifier Installation, Repair and Service

Do You Need a Humidifier or a Dehumidifier?

Some houses need both, at different times of year. Here’s the short version:

A cheap hygrometer from the hardware store will tell you where you actually sit. Under 30 percent or over 60 is where problems start.

A whole-home humidifier mounts on your ductwork or furnace and adds moisture to the air as it’s heated, so every room gets it. Bypass models are the common choice and cost less. Steam models cost more but add moisture faster and work even when the furnace isn’t running, which suits larger or tighter homes. Fan-powered models sit in between.

Installing one means cutting into the duct or plenum, running a small water line, setting up the drain and wiring the humidistat. The water line is why this is a plumber-and-HVAC job, and it’s one reason having both trades in one company helps.

Your AC removes some moisture on its own, but only while it’s running. On a mild, humid day it cycles off before it has pulled much out, which is when a house feels clammy at a comfortable temperature. A whole-house dehumidifier works independently of the cooling cycle and holds the level steady.

It ties into the ductwork, usually on the return side, and drains to a condensate line or pump. Oversizing causes short-cycling, so we size it to your square footage and how much moisture the house actually holds.

The reason this pays for itself: humid air feels warmer than dry air at the same temperature. Pull the humidity down and most people are comfortable a couple of degrees higher on the thermostat, which is a lower bill for the same comfort. In winter it runs the other way. Moist air holds heat better, so you’re comfortable at a slightly lower setting.

The dryness damage is the part people underestimate. Hardwood floors, cabinets and furniture shrink and crack in air that’s too dry, and pianos and instruments suffer. Too much moisture goes after drywall, insulation and the HVAC equipment itself.

The most common calls: a humidifier that’s stopped adding moisture because the water panel is clogged with mineral scale (DFW’s hard water makes that quick), a clogged or disconnected drain line leaving water where it shouldn’t be, a humidistat reading the wrong level, a solenoid valve that won’t open, or a dehumidifier running constantly and never reaching its setting.

Maintenance is light. Change the water panel once or twice a year, clean the drain line, check the humidistat against a separate meter, and have the drain and sensors looked at during your regular HVAC visit. We can roll it into a seasonal check so it isn’t a separate appointment.

Questions / Answers

GOOD TO
KNOW.

How do I know if my home needs a humidifier or a dehumidifier?

Pick up a hygrometer and check. Under 30 percent is too dry, over 60 is too damp. Or go by symptoms: static shocks and cracked wood mean dry, while a clammy feeling and window condensation mean damp. Plenty of DFW homes get both across a year.

What are the benefits of a whole-home system compared to portable units?

A portable unit treats one room and needs filling or emptying. A whole-home system treats the entire house through the ductwork, drains on its own and runs off a humidistat instead of your memory. It costs more up front and much less in hassle.

Can a whole-house dehumidifier help prevent mold?

It helps a lot. Mold needs moisture, and keeping relative humidity under about 60 percent takes away what it needs. It won’t fix an active leak or a wet crawl space, though. Those get repaired first.

How do smart thermostats work with these systems?

Many humidifiers and dehumidifiers wire into a compatible thermostat, so you set a humidity target the same way you set a temperature. Some adjust automatically as the outdoor temperature drops, which keeps you from overshooting and fogging up the windows.

What maintenance is required after installation?

For a humidifier, change the water panel once or twice a year and keep the drain line clear. Hard water here means the panel scales up faster than the box suggests. For a dehumidifier, clean the filter and check the drain. Both are quick, and we can fold them into a seasonal HVAC visit.

Are these systems safe for my HVAC equipment?

Yes, when they’re installed right. Balanced humidity is actually easier on the system. The risk comes from a bad install: a drain that isn’t pitched properly or a humidistat set too high can put moisture where you don’t want it.

Will this help with allergy symptoms?

It can. Dust mites and mold both need humidity above roughly 50 percent, so bringing the level down limits them. Very dry air irritates airways too. Controlling humidity works best alongside good filtration rather than instead of it.

How long does installation take?

Most whole-home humidifier installs take a few hours. A dehumidifier can take longer, depending on where it goes and how the ducts and drain run. We’ll give you a time frame with the quote.
