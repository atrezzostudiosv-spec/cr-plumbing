Questions / Answers

GOOD TO
KNOW.

Should I get a standby generator or a portable one?

Standby if you want automatic, zero-effort whole-home backup and don’t mind the higher upfront cost. Portable if you want a lower-cost option and don’t mind starting it manually during an outage. See the comparison table above for the full breakdown.

How big of a generator do I need for my house?

It depends on what you want powered during an outage — just essentials (fridge, sump pump, some lights) or the entire house including HVAC. We run a load calculation on your actual home rather than guessing off square footage, since that’s the difference between a generator that works and one that trips under load.

Can I connect a portable generator myself?

Not safely, and not legally in most cases. Plugging a portable generator directly into your home’s wiring or outlets risks backfeed — sending power back through the grid, which can seriously injure utility workers — plus fire and shock risk in your own home. A licensed electrician needs to install a transfer switch or inlet box.

What’s the difference between a manual and automatic transfer switch?

An automatic transfer switch (used with standby generators) detects an outage and switches your home to generator power on its own, no action needed. A manual transfer switch (used with portable generators) requires you to physically switch it over yourself.

Does a Generac generator run on natural gas or propane?

Either — Generac systems can run on natural gas (no refueling, tied to your gas line) or propane (stored fuel, useful if you don’t have gas service). We handle the integration for both.

Why does it matter if you're a Factory Authorized Generac dealer?

Because installation quality affects your warranty. A Factory Authorized dealer is trained and certified directly by Generac — improper installation by an unauthorized installer can void warranty coverage you’re paying for.

Will a generator power my entire home?

A properly sized standby generator can. A portable generator typically can’t — it powers the specific circuits you connect it to through a transfer switch, not the whole panel.

Do standby generators need regular maintenance?

Yes. Battery, oil, and filter checks plus a periodic load test keep it ready to actually start when the power goes out. A generator that sits untested for years is the most common reason one fails during an outage.

How long does generator installation take?

Standby installation typically involves a site assessment, permitting, the physical install, and final inspection. Portable generator connections (transfer switch or inlet box) are usually a single visit.

Do you install generators for businesses?

Yes — commercial and light commercial generator installation, sized for business continuity needs rather than residential load.

Do you service generators you didn’t install?

Yes, on most major brands, not just Generac or units we installed originally.
