Frequently asked questions

GOOD TO
KNOW.

How often should smoke and CO detectors be replaced?

Most smoke detectors should be replaced every 8 to 10 years, while CO detectors typically last 5 to 7 years depending on the model. Over time, sensors weaken and become less reliable. Even if the device still beeps during tests, it may not detect danger quickly. Replacing them on schedule is essential for dependable home protection.

Why is hardwired installation safer than battery-only detectors?

Hardwired detectors receive constant power from your home’s electrical system, ensuring they remain active at all times. They also include battery backup for protection during outages. Hardwired units can be interconnected, meaning if one goes off, all alarms in the home sound. This provides faster and more widespread warning during emergencies.

Where should smoke detectors be installed in a home?

Smoke detectors should be placed in every bedroom, outside sleeping areas and on every level of the home including basements. They should also be installed near stairways and central hallways for optimal coverage. High ceilings and open spaces may require additional detectors. Our technicians install them according to modern fire safety codes for maximum protection.

Where do carbon monoxide detectors need to be installed?

CO detectors should be installed near sleeping areas and on each level of the home. Homes with gas appliances, fireplaces, attached garages or fuel-burning heaters especially require proper CO monitoring. They should never be placed directly above gas appliances or too close to fuel sources. Correct placement ensures accurate detection and fewer false alarms.

Why are dual-sensor smoke detectors recommended?

Dual-sensor alarms combine photoelectric and ionization technology to detect both slow-smoldering fires and fast-flaming fires. This ensures more complete protection and quicker response across different fire types. Single-sensor units may fail to detect certain fire conditions fast enough. Dual-sensor detectors offer the most reliable all-around coverage.

How do interconnected detectors work?

When one interconnected detector senses smoke or CO, all connected alarms in the home activate simultaneously. This ensures everyone in the house hears the alarm, even if the danger starts far from their room. Interconnection can be achieved through hardwiring or wireless network systems. It’s one of the most effective upgrades for larger or multi-level homes.

Can I install smoke and CO detectors myself?

While DIY installation is possible, incorrect placement or wiring can reduce the device’s effectiveness. Hardwired systems require proper electrical work, grounding and code compliance. A licensed electrician ensures each detector is installed in the right location and functions exactly as intended. Professional installation ensures long term safety and reliability.

How often should I test smoke and CO detectors?

Both detectors should be tested monthly using the built-in test button. Batteries should be replaced once a year unless the unit uses long-life sealed batteries. Regular testing ensures the alarm mechanism and sensors respond correctly. We also recommend an annual inspection to verify full system performance and interconnection.
