Frequently asked questions

GOOD TO
KNOW.

How often should smoke and CO detectors be inspected?

Smoke and carbon monoxide detectors should be inspected at least once a month to ensure they are working properly. In addition to homeowner checks, a professional smoke detector inspection is recommended annually to verify wiring, sensor accuracy, and battery backup systems. Regular testing ensures early warning protection in case of fire or gas leaks.

How do I know if my detectors need to be replaced?

Most smoke and CO detectors need replacement every 7–10 years, depending on manufacturer guidelines. Signs like frequent false alarms, weak alerts, or non-responsive test buttons indicate it’s time for a replacement. A licensed technician can confirm whether repair or full detector replacement services are needed.

What happens during a professional detector inspection?

A professional inspection includes testing alarm sound levels, checking sensor response, verifying battery or hardwired power, and ensuring correct placement. Electricians also inspect wiring connections and system interconnectivity for full-home coverage. This ensures your smoke and CO safety systems meet current safety standards.

Why are smoke and CO detectors required in specific locations?

Detectors are placed in specific areas like bedrooms, hallways, kitchens, and near fuel-burning appliances to provide early warning where risk is highest. Carbon monoxide detectors are especially important near garages, furnaces, and gas appliances. Proper placement ensures maximum protection and compliance with safety codes.

What types of detectors should homeowners use?

Homeowners should use a combination of smoke detectors (ionization and photoelectric) and carbon monoxide detectors for complete safety coverage. Hardwired interconnected systems are preferred for whole-home protection. A professional smoke and CO detector installation ensures proper selection and placement.

Can detectors be interconnected?

Yes, interconnected detectors are designed so that when one alarm is triggered, all alarms in the home sound simultaneously. This provides faster warning across all rooms, improving safety during emergencies. Modern interconnected smoke detector systems can be hardwired or wireless for full-home coverage.

What are the signs that a detector isn’t working properly?

Common signs include frequent false alarms, no sound during testing, blinking error lights, or weak alarm volume. If a detector does not respond to smoke or test signals, it may have sensor failure or power issues. In such cases, immediate smoke detector repair or replacement is recommended.

Are smart smoke and CO detectors worth upgrading to?

Yes, smart smoke and CO detectors offer advanced features like mobile alerts, voice notifications, and system integration with smart home platforms. They provide real-time updates even when you are away from home, improving safety response time. A professional smart detector installation ensures proper setup and connectivity.
