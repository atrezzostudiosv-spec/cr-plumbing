Electrical Code Inspection & Correction in DFW

Failed an inspection, or got a home inspection report full of electrical items? We fix the violations and get the work ready to pass. Serving Argyle, Fort Worth, Flower Mound, North Richland Hills, Denton and Southlake.

What Code Correction Actually Involves

Most people find us one of two ways. A city inspector failed a job and left a correction notice. Or a home inspector’s report came back during a sale with a page of electrical items on it. Either way, you’ve got a list and usually a deadline. We go through the list item by item, check each one in person, and fix what’s there.

Common Electrical Code Violations We Fix

Some violations turn up on almost every report we see. Double-tapped breakers, where two wires share a terminal built for one. Missing GFCI protection in kitchens, bathrooms, garages and outdoors. Three-prong outlets with no ground wire behind them. Splices sitting loose in the attic instead of inside a junction box.

All Code Inspection & Correction Services We Offer

Code Inspection & Correction Professional electrical code compliance evaluations identify violations of current Texas electrical standards, followed by expert corrective services that bring outdated systems into full compliance with modern safety requirements, local building codes, and insurance standards while coordinating all necessary permits and inspections.

QUESTIONS & ANSWERS

What is an electrical code inspection?

It’s a check of your wiring, panel, outlets and safety devices against the electrical code your city enforces. The city does one whenever permitted work is done. A home inspector does a lighter version during a sale. We can also do one for you before either of those happens.

When should homeowners schedule a code inspection?

Before you sell, after you buy, before a big remodel, or after someone did electrical work in the house without a permit. Problems are a lot easier to fix on your schedule than on a closing deadline.

What types of issues are commonly found during code inspections?

Double-tapped breakers, missing GFCI or AFCI protection, ungrounded outlets, open splices, reversed polarity, and wire that’s too small for its breaker.

Will the inspection disrupt my home or require wall openings?

Usually not much. Most corrections happen at the panel, outlets and junction boxes. If we do need to open a wall or ceiling, we’ll tell you first and keep the opening as small as we can.

Do you handle permits for code corrections?

Yes. If a correction needs a permit, we pull it and schedule the inspection.

How long does code correction work usually take?

A handful of items off a home inspection report can often be done in a day. A longer correction list, or anything involving the panel, takes more time, partly depending on when the city can come inspect.

What if my electrical system doesn’t pass inspection?

The inspector notes what’s still wrong. We fix it and schedule the re-inspection. Most re-inspections are for small items.
