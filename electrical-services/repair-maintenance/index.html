03 / Electrical

Electrical Repair and Maintenance Services

Here’s what we get called out for most, and what’s usually behind each one.

A breaker trips to protect the circuit, so it’s doing its job. The question is why. Usually it’s too much load on one circuit, a loose connection, or a failing device somewhere down the line. If it’s an AFCI or GFCI breaker, it’s often sensing damaged wire or moisture.

Resetting it over and over doesn’t fix anything. We test the circuit under load, find the fault, and repair it.

When a few outlets quit at once and the breaker looks fine, there’s usually a tripped GFCI upstream or a backstabbed connection that’s worked loose. Those loose connections heat up over time, which is why a warm outlet or a faint burning smell is worth a call right away.

We repair or replace the device, check the rest of the circuit, and look at what caused it.

Lights that flicker in one fixture usually mean a loose bulb or a bad switch. Lights that dim across the whole house when the AC or a well pump kicks on point to something bigger, like a loose neutral, an undersized service or a failing connection at the panel or meter.

The whole-house version is the one to take seriously. A loose neutral can damage electronics and appliances.

We repair and replace breakers, tighten and clean connections, and replace panels that are rusted, undersized or no longer safe. If your panel is one of the older brands that insurers flag, we’ll tell you and explain the options.

Before quoting a panel upgrade, we do a load calculation. Sometimes you don’t need one. A dedicated circuit or a load management device can be enough, and it costs a lot less.

Maintenance is mostly about catching heat and movement early. We check the panel for loose lugs and discoloration, test GFCI and AFCI devices, check grounding and bonding, look at outdoor outlets and fixtures, and test smoke and CO detectors.

For businesses, we can set this up on a schedule, which helps with insurance requirements and keeps a paper trail of what’s been checked.

Frequently asked questions

GOOD TO
KNOW.

What qualifies as a general electrical repair in my home?

Anything that involves fixing existing wiring, devices or protection. Dead outlets, bad switches, tripping breakers, damaged wire, loose connections, failing light fixtures and panel repairs all fall under it. New installs and upgrades are a separate category.

When should I call for general electrical services instead of waiting?

Call right away for burning smells, scorch marks, warm outlets or cover plates, sparking, a hot panel, or repeated breaker trips on the same circuit. Those point to heat somewhere in the system. Flickering lights and the occasional dead outlet can wait a day or two, but not a month.

Why does my breaker keep tripping more than it used to?

Usually one of three things. You’ve added load to that circuit, a connection has worked loose, or a device on the circuit is failing. An older breaker can also weaken and trip at less than its rating. Testing the circuit under load tells us which it is.

What are the signs my home may have outdated wiring?

Two-prong outlets with no ground, a fuse box instead of breakers, cloth or rubber-sheathed wire visible in the attic or basement, no GFCI protection in kitchens and baths, and a 60 or 100-amp service in a house running modern appliances. Any one of these is worth an inspection.

Is underground wiring harder to repair than indoor wiring?

It takes more work to reach, yes. Finding the fault means tracing the line and then digging at the right spot. The repair itself is straightforward once it’s exposed. We use direct-burial rated wire and conduit so the fix holds up.

What causes a sudden electrical outage in a single room?

Most often a tripped GFCI outlet somewhere else on the circuit, like a garage or bathroom, since one GFCI can protect several rooms. After that, it’s usually a loose connection in an outlet or switch box. Check your GFCI outlets and press reset before you call.

How often should electrical maintenance be scheduled?

Once every few years for most homes, and yearly for homes over 40 years old or businesses with heavy equipment. Also worth doing when you buy a house, after a remodel, or after storm or flood damage.

What should I do if I notice a burning smell from an outlet or switch?

Stop using it, turn off the breaker for that circuit, and call an electrician. A burning smell means something is overheating behind the wall. If you see smoke or scorch marks, shut off the main breaker if it’s safe to reach and call 911 first.
