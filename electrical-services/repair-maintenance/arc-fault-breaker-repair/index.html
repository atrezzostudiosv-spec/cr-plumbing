Why Arc Fault Breakers Keep Tripping

Most calls start the same way. A bedroom or living room goes dead, you flip the breaker back on, and it trips again. Sometimes right away. Sometimes an hour later when somebody turns on the vacuum.

There are really three possibilities. The breaker itself could be worn out or defective, which does happen, especially with older AFCIs. Something plugged into the circuit could be setting it off; vacuums, treadmills, space heaters and older power tools are all known for it. Or there’s a real wiring problem , like a loose connection at an outlet, a nail through a wire, or a neutral shared with another circuit.

The first two are quick fixes. The third is the one that matters, because it’s exactly what the breaker is there to catch. We test before we replace anything, so you know which one you’re dealing with.

03 / Electrical

Arc Fault Breaker Replacement

Sometimes the breaker really is the problem. AFCIs have electronics inside, and electronics fail. Older models are also touchier than current ones and can trip on things that aren’t actually a hazard.

When we replace an arc fault breaker, we match it to your panel’s brand and the circuit’s rating. Not every AFCI fits every panel, and using the wrong one can void the panel’s listing.

If the circuit also needs GFCI protection, which is common in kitchens and laundry rooms, we may recommend a dual function breaker instead. It handles both jobs in one.

Plenty of older homes don’t have AFCI protection at all. That’s not a violation on its own, since existing homes don’t have to update every time the code changes. But if you’re remodeling or extending a circuit, code usually requires adding it at that point, and our licensed electricians can add it to other circuits too if you want the protection.

Frequently asked questions

GOOD TO
KNOW.

What exactly does an arc fault breaker do?

It watches the current on a circuit for the pattern that arcing creates, from a damaged wire or loose connection for example, and shuts the circuit off before it can start a fire. A regular breaker only trips on overloads and short circuits, so it can miss arcing completely.

How do I know if my arc fault breaker is malfunctioning?

If it trips with nothing plugged in, won’t reset at all, or trips at random with no pattern, the breaker itself may be bad. If it only trips when one particular thing is running, that device is the more likely cause.

What causes arc faults inside a home’s electrical system?

Damaged cords, loose connections at outlets or switches, nails or screws driven through wiring, worn insulation in older homes, and cords pinched under furniture or doors.

Can I reset an arc fault breaker myself?

Yes, once. Unplug everything on that circuit, flip the breaker fully off, then back on. If it trips again right away, or keeps tripping over the next day or two, leave it off and call us. Resetting it over and over just hides the problem.

Why are arc fault breakers required by electrical codes?

Because arcing is a common cause of house fires, and a standard breaker doesn’t reliably catch it. The National Electrical Code started requiring AFCIs in bedrooms in the early 2000s and has expanded that to most living areas since.

How is arc fault breaker repair different from general electric repair?

An AFCI problem could be the breaker, a device or the wiring, so it takes more testing to pin down than most repairs. There are also specific code rules about which circuits need the protection.

What should I expect during an AFCI breaker repair appointment?

We’ll ask when it trips and what’s usually running, test the breaker, then check the outlets and devices on that circuit. Once we know the cause, you get a price, we fix it, and we test it again before we leave.

When should I call a professional for AFCI issues?

If it trips more than once, won’t reset, or you notice warm outlets, flickering lights or a burning smell anywhere on that circuit.

Can I replace an arc fault breaker with a regular breaker?

Not where code calls for AFCI protection, which is most living areas in a newer home. A standard breaker will stop the tripping, but only because it’s no longer looking for the problem. It also won’t pass inspection.

How much does it cost to replace an AFCI breaker?

An AFCI breaker costs more than a standard one, and a dual function AFCI/GFCI breaker costs more again. The bigger question is whether the breaker is really the problem. We test first and give you a price before we replace anything.
