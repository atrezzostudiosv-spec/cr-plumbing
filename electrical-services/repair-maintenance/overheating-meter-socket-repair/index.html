01

Emergency Priority

A meter base that's scorched, buzzing or melting goes to the top of our list. Heat at the meter can turn into a fire, so we treat it like one.

02

Utility Coordination

The utility owns the meter. You own the base it sits in. Before anyone can work on the base, the utility has to disconnect service. We set that up with Oncor, CoServ or whoever serves your address, so you don't have to.

03

Thermal Analysis

A thermal camera shows us exactly where the heat is: the meter jaws, a lug, or the service wires coming in. That tells us whether it's a repair or a full replacement.

04

Code Compliance

Replacements are built to current code, and most need a city inspection before the utility will reconnect. We pull the permit and handle the inspection.

02 / Electrical

What Causes a Meter Socket to Overheat

Usually it’s a loose connection. The meter plugs into spring-loaded jaws inside the base, and over the years those jaws lose their grip. Add a Texas summer, with the meter box baking in direct sun, and it gets worse. A weak contact creates resistance, resistance creates heat, and the heat weakens the contact even more. It feeds itself.

Corrosion does the same thing, especially on older meter bases where water has worked its way in around the meter ring. In some homes the trouble is the service wires coming in, either undersized for what the house draws today or never tightened properly to begin with.

Things to watch for: burn marks or discoloration around the meter, melted plastic, buzzing or crackling from the box, lights that flicker for no clear reason, or siding that feels hot near the meter. If you notice any of that, call us. Don’t try to pull the meter or open the base yourself.

01

Emergency Electrical Repair Services

Scorch marks, melting or a burning smell at the meter? We’ll come out, make it safe, and get the utility scheduled to disconnect so the base can be fixed.

02

Accurate Diagnosis & Fault Detection

We check the meter jaws, the lugs, the service entrance wires and the connections to your panel, so we fix the cause and not just the part that looks burned.

03

Professional Repairs & Component Replacement

If the damage is limited, we repair the jaws or connections. If the base is burned, corroded or just too old, we replace it, along with any service wires that took heat damage. If the panel was affected too, we’ll tell you.

04

Electrical Inspection

Beyond immediate repairs, we inspect your system for potential risks and recommend preventive solutions. Regular inspections and timely repairs help avoid future overheating issues and maintain a reliable electrical system.

03 / Electrical

Meter Base Replacement

A lot of the time, a burned meter socket can’t be repaired. Once heat has taken the tension out of the jaws, or the plastic has started to melt, the only safe fix is replacing the meter base. It’s a bigger job than a repair, but it’s a routine one for us.

Here’s how it usually goes. We look at the damage and pull the permit. The utility disconnects service. We take out the old base, install the new one, reconnect the service entrance and the feed to your panel, and torque everything to spec. The city inspects it, and once it passes, the utility sets the meter and turns the power back on. Most homes are without power for part of a day, sometimes longer, depending on how quickly the utility can reconnect.

While the base is open, we look at what’s around it. Older homes sometimes have service wires or a panel that are just as worn out as the meter base, and it’s better to find that out now than after the next outage.

Our electrical service team handles meter base and meter box replacement for homes and small commercial buildings across North Texas.

Frequently asked questions

GOOD TO
KNOW.

What are the warning signs of an overheating meter socket?

Burn marks or discoloration around the meter, melted plastic, buzzing or crackling from the box, flickering lights, or heat you can feel on the siding. A burning smell near the meter is the most urgent one.

What causes a meter socket to overheat?

Loose connections between the meter and the socket jaws, corrosion, years of heating and cooling, and sometimes service wires that were never tightened properly or can’t handle what the house uses now.

Is an overheating meter socket dangerous?

Yes. A hot socket can start a fire on the outside of your house, and it gets worse over time, not better. Treat it as urgent.

Can I shut off power myself if my meter socket overheats?

You can turn off the main breaker in your panel, which takes the load off the meter. But the wires coming in from the utility stay live all the way to the meter base. Don’t pull the meter or open the box. Call us, and if there’s smoke or fire, call 911.

How do electricians diagnose overheating meter sockets?

We start with a visual check and a thermal camera to find the hottest point. Once the utility has made it safe to open, we look at the meter jaws, lugs and service wires up close.

Will the utility company need to be involved in the repair?

Yes. The utility owns the meter and has to disconnect service before anyone can work on the base. We coordinate that for you.

How soon should I call for repair if I suspect overheating?

Right away. If you see melting, smell burning or hear buzzing at the meter, call the same day.

Does repairing an overheating meter socket fall under general electrical services?

It’s an electrical repair, but a specialized one. It involves the utility, usually a permit, and wires that can’t be shut off from inside the house, so it needs a licensed electrician.

Who is responsible for the electric meter box, me or the utility?

Usually the utility owns the meter itself, and the homeowner owns the meter base (the box it plugs into) plus the wiring into the house. So if the base is damaged, the repair is normally the homeowner’s. Your utility can confirm how it works at your address.
