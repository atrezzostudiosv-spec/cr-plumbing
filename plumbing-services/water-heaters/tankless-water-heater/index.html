Tankless Water Heater Services for DFW Homes

A tank heater keeps 40 or 50 gallons hot around the clock, whether anyone’s home or not. A tankless unit skips all that. When you open a hot tap, water runs through a heat exchanger, a gas burner or electric elements fire up, and the water comes out hot until you shut it off. No tank, no standby heat loss, and no cold shower because two people showered back to back.

There’s one thing every tankless owner in North Texas should know. Our water is hard, and minerals build up inside the heat exchanger over time. That restricts flow, makes the temperature swing and eventually trips an error code. It’s the most common reason a tankless unit starts acting up here, and a yearly descaling prevents most of it.

We handle the whole job: installation, repair and maintenance. We’ll also tell you when tankless isn’t the right call. In some homes, a conventional tank water heater is simpler and costs less. Our water heater services page covers both.

The big one is water that’s lukewarm or swings between hot and cold mid-shower. After that, look for an error code on the display, hot water that takes forever to arrive, a unit that clicks and tries to fire but doesn’t, or reduced flow at the tap. Most of these trace back to scale or a sensor problem, and most are fixable. One safety note: if you smell gas near a gas unit, leave the house and call your gas utility first.

Tankless units are compact, but there’s a lot going on inside them. A tankless water heater technician has to read error codes, check flow and gas pressure and know what each sensor does. The usual suspects are scale in the heat exchanger, a clogged inlet filter, a dirty flame sensor, a blocked vent or condensate drain, and a failed flow sensor or thermistor. Then there’s the “cold water sandwich,” where you get a short blast of cold between two hot draws. That’s often a flow or setting problem, not a defective unit.

Bring us the code if there’s one. We’ll find the cause and tell you what it takes to fix. If the heat exchanger has failed on an older unit, we’ll tell you that too.

The unit you choose matters less than getting it sized right. We look at how many fixtures run at the same time (a shower, a dishwasher and a washing machine, say) and how much the water has to be heated. Water here arrives warmer than in colder states, so a unit can often keep up with less heating power than you’d expect. Questions / Answers

GOOD TO
KNOW.

How does a tankless water heater work?

When you open a hot tap, a flow sensor notices the water moving and fires the unit. A gas burner or electric elements heat the water as it passes through a heat exchanger, and the unit shuts off when you close the tap. There’s no stored water, so nothing sits around heated.

What are the benefits of switching to a tankless water heater?

Hot water that doesn’t run out, no standby heat loss, a unit that mounts on the wall, and a longer life, typically 15 to 20 years against 8 to 12 for a tank. The tradeoffs are a higher install cost and the yearly maintenance that hard water calls for.

How long do tankless water heaters typically last?

Around 15 to 20 years if they’re flushed every year. In hard water, skipping that can cut it short.

Can a tankless water heater supply enough hot water for my whole home?

Yes, if it’s sized right. A tankless unit is limited by flow, in gallons per minute, not by tank size. A properly sized one handles several showers at once, but an undersized one will struggle. We size it by what runs at the same time in your house. Very large homes sometimes use two units.

What maintenance does a tankless system require?

Once a year, we flush the heat exchanger with a descaling solution, clean the inlet filter and check the vent and condensate drain. In hard water, that’s the one thing that matters most.

Will a tankless water heater reduce my energy bills?

Usually, yes. There’s no tank to keep reheating all day. How much you save depends on how much hot water you use, and lower-use households tend to save more. For a very high-use home, the difference is smaller.

Should I choose gas or electric?

If your home has gas, a gas unit is the usual choice for whole-home hot water. Electric units work for smaller homes or places without gas, but they need a lot of electrical capacity. The table above shows what each one asks of your house.

Why is my tankless water heater showing an error code?

Codes point to a problem with ignition, flow, a sensor, the vent or scale. Check the code on the display or app, turn the unit off and on once, and if it comes back, call us. If you smell gas, leave the house and call your gas utility first.
