Sump Pump Services for North Texas Homes

Most DFW homes sit on a slab, so full basements are the exception here. That doesn’t mean water isn’t a problem. Our clay soil doesn’t soak up rain. It sheds it, and the water collects in low spots: under pier-and-beam floors, in garage pits and against foundation walls. A sump pump gives that water somewhere to go.

It’s simple in principle. Water drains into a pit, a float rises, and the pump pushes it out through a discharge line and away from the house. When that works, you forget it’s there. When it doesn’t, you find out with a wet crawl space, a musty smell or a foundation that’s taking on water.

Most of the calls we get are repairs. A float switch gets stuck. A motor burns out. A check valve fails and the water the pump just moved drains right back into the pit. Or the discharge line freezes, cracks or clogs. We’ll find what’s wrong, fix it if it’s worth fixing and replace the pump if it isn’t. You’ll hear which one it is before we start.

We also install new sump pumps and outdoor drainage pumps, and we work with the rest of your plumbing, from water lines to the sewer line. Sump pump work is one part of the wider plumbing services we offer across DFW.

GOOD TO
KNOW.

What does a sump pump do and why is it important?

It sits in a pit at the lowest spot where water collects. When the water rises, a float switch turns the pump on and it pushes the water out through a discharge line. Here in North Texas that mostly means protecting crawl spaces and foundations, because our clay soil holds rainwater instead of draining it.

How do I know if my sump pump needs repair or replacement?

Watch for a pump that runs constantly, won’t turn on, makes loud grinding or rattling noises, or leaves the pit full after a storm. Quick test: pour a bucket of water into the pit. If the pump doesn’t kick on, give us a call. Most pumps last around 7 to 10 years, so an older one with any of these problems is often cheaper to replace than repair.

What types of sump pumps do you install and service?

Submersible pumps, which sit inside the pit and run quieter, and pedestal pumps, which sit above the pit and are easier to get to. We also install battery backup pumps that keep going when the power’s out, and outdoor pumps for yards and low spots that flood.

How often should a sump pump be maintained?

Once a year, ideally before spring storm season. In between, test it yourself every few months with a bucket of water. Takes about a minute.

Do you offer emergency sump pump repairs?

Yes. If your pump quit and the water’s rising, call (940) 370-5663 and we’ll get someone out as fast as we can.

What areas do you serve for sump pump installation and repairs?

Argyle, Flower Mound, Denton, Fort Worth, Lewisville, Carrollton, Coppell, The Colony, Grapevine, Irving, Grand Prairie, Southlake, North Richland Hills and the towns around them.

Do I need a sump pump if my house doesn't have a basement?

Maybe. Plenty of DFW homes without basements still use one. If water pools in your crawl space, collects against the foundation, or turns part of the yard into a pond every time it rains, a crawl space or outdoor sump pump can fix that. We’ll come look and tell you honestly whether you need one.
