Sewer Line Services for DFW Homes

Your sewer line is the pipe you never think about until it fails. It runs from your house to the city main or septic tank, usually buried a few feet down, and it handles everything that goes down every drain you own. When it cracks, sags or fills with roots, the whole house feels it. North Texas is rough on these pipes. Expansive clay swells and shrinks with the weather and slowly pulls joints apart. Older homes still have clay or cast iron lines that corrode and flake. Tree roots find every small gap and move in. So a lot of the sewer work we do starts with the same call: “Something’s wrong, but I don’t know what.”

That’s where we start too. We run a camera down the line, find the problem and show you the footage. Sometimes it’s a clog that needs clearing and a repair. Sometimes the pipe is shot and has to be replaced. If the damage allows, trenchless sewer repair fixes it without trenching across your yard. We’ll explain each option, what it costs and what we’d do if it were our house. Sewer line work is one part of the plumbing services we offer across DFW, so if the real problem turns out to be somewhere else in the system, we’ll tell you.

Sewer Inspection, Repair and Replacement

One slow drain is usually just a clog. Several at once is a different story. Watch for gurgling toilets, water backing up in the lowest tub or shower, a sewage smell inside or out, and lush green patches or soft spots in the yard along the line’s path. Foundation cracks and unexplained pest problems can also point to a broken line. If you see two or more of these, call before it turns into a backup.

This is where every job starts. You might hear it called a sewer scope, a sewer camera inspection or a video inspection. It’s all the same thing. We feed a waterproof camera through the cleanout and follow the pipe to the street, watching for cracks, offset joints, sagging sections and roots. The locator on the camera tells us exactly where and how deep a problem sits. That means the repair covers just the damaged section and nothing more. If you’re buying a home, an inspection before closing is cheap insurance. Older neighborhoods in particular can hide a failing line for years.

The big four are roots, shifting soil, age and what gets flushed. Roots squeeze in through joints and grow into a mat that catches everything. Clay soil moves with rainfall and drought, which cracks rigid pipe and pulls joints apart. Cast iron and clay pipe from older homes corrodes or crumbles. And “flushable” wipes really aren’t. They snag on rough spots and build up fast.

If the damage is limited to one section, spot repair is often the right call. We dig up or cut into just that stretch, replace it and reconnect to the existing line. Stubborn clogs may need hydro jetting first. For routine cleaning and the repairs that follow, see our page on sewer line repair and maintenance.

Trenchless (or no-dig) methods fix the pipe from the inside, or pull a new one through the old one, so you don’t need a long trench. It works well when the line is mostly intact and you’d rather not lose a driveway, patio or mature trees. It isn’t right for every pipe, since a badly collapsed or bellied line may still need digging. Get the full rundown on trenchless sewer repair.

Sometimes repair is just money thrown at a failing pipe. If the line is cracked in several places, collapsed, bellied or made of material that’s reached the end of its life, replacing it is the better long-term fix. We replace sewer lines using traditional excavation or trenchless pipe bursting, depending on the layout of your property and what the camera shows. You’ll get both prices before you decide.

Questions / Answers

GOOD TO
KNOW.

How do I know if I have a sewer line issue?

Look for more than one drain acting up at once, gurgling when you flush or run the washer, a backup in the lowest tub or shower, or a sewage smell. Wet or unusually green spots in the yard along the line are another sign. A camera inspection tells you for sure.

What causes sewer line damage?

Mostly tree roots, shifting clay soil and age. Older cast iron and clay pipes corrode or crack. Flushing wipes, grease and “flushable” products speeds up the buildup.

Do you offer trenchless sewer repair?

Yes, when the pipe allows it. You may see it called no-dig sewer repair. Same thing. It works best on lines that are cracked or root-damaged but still in place. If the pipe has collapsed, we’ll tell you straight that digging is the better fix. More on that on our trenchless sewer repair page.

Can you work around my landscaping?

We do our best. Trenchless repair avoids most of the yard. When we do have to dig, we plan the route to miss trees, sprinkler lines and hardscape where we can, and we’ll go over it with you first.

Do you provide a free quote?

Yes, you can request a free quote by calling or scheduling an inspection online. We typically begin with a camera inspection to assess the issue and provide an accurate estimate for your sewer line services. This ensures transparent pricing with no surprises.

Are permits required?

Often, yes. Sewer line replacement usually needs a permit and an inspection, and the rules differ by city. Check local permit and inspection requirements, and confirm permit responsibilities with CR before scheduling work.

What’s the average cost of repair?

It swings a lot. A short spot repair costs far less than replacing a long line, and depth, access and pipe material all change the number. That’s why we camera the line first and give you a written price for each option before you decide.

How long do repairs take?

A spot repair is often done in a day. Trenchless lining or pipe bursting typically takes a day or two. Full replacement with trenching can take longer, depending on length and weather. We’ll give you a realistic timeline with the quote.
