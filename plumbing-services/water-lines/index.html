Questions / Answers

GOOD TO
KNOW.

How do I know if my water line is leaking?

Signs of a water line leak include unexplained increases in your water bill, low water pressure, wet spots in your yard, foundation cracks, or the sound of running water when no fixtures are in use. A professional water line inspection can quickly identify the source and prevent further damage.

Can you repair a water line without digging up my yard?

Yes, we offer trenchless water line repair for many pipe types and conditions. This method minimizes excavation by repairing or replacing the line with minimal disruption to your landscaping, making it a faster and cleaner solution compared to traditional digging.

How much does a water line repair cost?

The cost of water line repair depends on factors like pipe size, depth, location, and the extent of the damage. We provide clear, upfront pricing after a detailed inspection so you know exactly what to expect before any work begins.

Is water line repair covered by homeowner’s insurance?

In some cases, water line damage may be covered by homeowner’s insurance, depending on your policy and the cause of the issue. We can provide detailed documentation and inspection reports to help support your insurance claim.

What areas do you provide water line services in?

We offer professional water line repair and replacement services across Argyle, Fort Worth, Flower Mound, Denton, North Richland Hills, Southlake, The Colony, Farmers Branch, Lewisville, Coppell, and Carrollton. Our team serves both residential properties and surrounding communities with reliable, local service.

How quickly can you respond to a water line issue?

For urgent water line leaks or breaks, we offer same-day or next-day service whenever possible. Fast response times help minimize water damage and restore your home’s plumbing system quickly.

Do you replace old or damaged water lines like galvanized or polybutylene pipes?

Yes, we specialize in water line replacement, including upgrading outdated materials such as galvanized or polybutylene pipes. Replacing old lines improves water quality, pressure, and overall system reliability for your home.
