Backflow Prevention Services That Keep You Compliant

Backflow sounds like a technical problem until you picture what it means. A pressure drop on the city main, say from a water main break or firefighters opening a hydrant, can pull water backward through your pipes. If a sprinkler head is sitting in fertilized soil, that’s what ends up in the line you drink from. A backflow preventer is the one-way valve that stops it.

The catch is that these devices wear out. Springs weaken, rubber checks harden and debris gets stuck in the seats, and none of that shows from the outside. That’s why most Texas cities require a licensed tester to check them, and why a lot of homeowners only hear about theirs when a compliance letter shows up.

That’s where we come in. We’re a backflow plumber, not just a testing company. If your device fails, the same tech can usually repair it on the spot instead of sending you off to find someone else. It’s part of the wider plumbing services we provide across DFW.

03 / Plumbing

Backflow Testing, Repair and Installation

Annual Backflow Testing and Inspection The test itself is quick. Our tester hooks a gauge kit to the test ports, checks each valve’s pressure differential and makes sure the device closes the way it’s supposed to. Most residential tests take well under an hour. You get a pass or fail on the spot. If it passes, the paperwork goes to your water provider or HOA. If it fails, we tell you exactly which part failed and what the fix costs. Looking at backflow testing companies? Ask whether they repair too. Some only test. That leaves you with a failed report and a second call to make. RPZ Inspections for Commercial Properties Restaurants, medical offices, car washes and buildings with boilers or fire systems usually need a reduced pressure zone (RPZ) assembly. It’s the highest level of protection, and it gets the strictest testing. We handle commercial RPZ inspections, retests after repairs and replacements when an old unit isn’t worth rebuilding. If your property has several devices, we can test them all in one visit and keep their due dates lined up. Backflow Repair A failed test usually means one of three things: a worn rubber check, a weak spring or debris stuck in a valve seat. Most of that can be fixed with a repair kit, not a whole new device. We repair it, retest it and send the passing result in. If water is pouring from the relief valve on an RPZ, don’t wait for your next test date. That’s a sign it’s already failing and wasting water. Backflow Preventer Installation New irrigation systems in Texas need a backflow preventer, and so do a lot of remodels and commercial build-outs. We’ll recommend the right device for the job, put it where it can be tested and serviced easily, and test it before we leave so it’s compliant from day one. We also install insulated covers. Questions / Answers

GOOD TO
KNOW.

What is a backflow preventer, and do I really need one?

It’s a valve that lets water flow into your property but never back out into the public supply. If you have a sprinkler system, you almost certainly have one already. Texas requires it on irrigation systems.

How often does a backflow device need to be tested?

Usually once a year, plus right after it’s installed or repaired. Your city or HOA sets the exact schedule. We’ll remind you when your next one’s coming up.

What happens if my backflow test fails?

Nothing dramatic. We’ll show you which part failed, repair it (often the same day) and retest it. Once it passes, the result goes in like any other test.

How long does a backflow test take?

A single residential device usually takes under an hour. Commercial sites with several RPZs take longer, mostly depending on how many devices there are and how easy they are to reach.

What types of backflow assemblies do you install and service?

RPZ, PVB and double check valve assemblies, for both homes and businesses. If you’re not sure which you have, the table above will help.

What are signs my backflow device needs repair?

Water dripping or gushing from the relief valve, low water pressure at the sprinklers, rust or cracks on the body, or a failed test. Any of those means it’s time to call.

Do you work with HOAs and commercial properties?

Yes. We test and repair devices for HOA common areas, restaurants, offices and multi-unit buildings, and we can put every device on one testing schedule.

What areas do you serve for backflow testing?

Fort Worth, Tarrant County and Denton County, including Denton, Keller, Watauga, Lewisville, Flower Mound, Southlake and Argyle.
